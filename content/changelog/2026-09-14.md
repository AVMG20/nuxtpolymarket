---
title: Polytown Needs Rebalanced
description: Townsfolk ask for far less bread, tools and luxuries, so a town can finally run a surplus.
date: 2026-09-14
---

- **Needs rebalanced** — the old rates asked for half the town to bake its bread and another half to forge its tools, so no town could supply its people and still have goods to sell. Each need is now priced against what a unit costs in residents to make; all of them together take about a fifth of the town, under a third once luxuries join.
- **Grain** — one a tick per 24 residents (was 12).
- **Bricks** — one per 120 residents from 40 residents (was one per 60 from 12).
- **Bread** — one per 160 residents from 120 residents (was one per 24 from 16).
- **Tools** — one per 300 residents from 200 residents (was one per 40 from 40).
- **Luxuries** — one per 4,000 residents from 3,000 residents (was one per 160 from 120).
- **Happiness redesigned** — parks no longer add to the town score just for existing. A park, bathhouse or theatre now cheers only the homes within its 4-tile reach, by a few points per level, and a single home gains at most +48 from all the parks around it together — exactly what a maxed park, bathhouse or theatre gives on its own. The bathhouse reaches a tile further than a park and the theatre three, so the later civic buildings cheer bigger neighbourhoods rather than taller numbers. The town feels the resident-weighted average of what its homes feel.
- **No more smog ceiling** — the industry penalty was capped at −25 for the whole town, so a few parks could paper over any factory district. The cap is gone: every workshop in reach of a home costs that home its full nuisance, without limit. Two miserable residents in a town of five hundred still barely move the needle, but a street of houses ringed by factories is exactly as unhappy as it looks, and no number of parks will buy that back.

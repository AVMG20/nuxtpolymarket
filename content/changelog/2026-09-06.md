---
title: Polytown
description: A new idle town builder — claim a plot on the endless grid, house your townsfolk, turn wheat into luxuries, and sell it all at the town hall or to other mayors.
date: 2026-09-06
---

- **Your plot** — every mayor starts with a free 8×8 plot. Houses bring residents, residents staff industry, and every industry tile makes the town a little less happy. Happiness sets how fast the whole town ticks, so parks and bread are not decoration.
- **Six tiers of goods** — wheat, wood and stone feed mills, sawmills and kilns; flour and planks become bread and tools; tools open the iron mine, steel, machines and finally luxuries. Each tier is gated on running the tier before it, and higher tiers cost the goods of the lower ones, not just coins.
- **Levels** — every building upgrades to level 20. Output, workers and effects scale with level; cost and build time climb with it.
- **Two markets** — the town hall always buys at a floor price and sells at a ceiling, so you can never be stuck with stock. Between the two sits a player order book per resource: list offers, fill crossing ones instantly at the resting price, watch the trades roll in live.
- **Land office** — more plots cost more and open more slowly: ten minutes for the second, tripling after that, up to twelve.
- **Milestones** — twenty one-time rewards that double as the guide from first house to first emporium.
- **Layout matters** — a house beside a park is a happier house; a house beside a quarry is not.
- **Welcome back** — the town keeps producing for up to eight hours while you are away, and tells you what it made when you return.
- **Rush** — one gem per five minutes left on any build or upgrade.
- **Mayors** — a leaderboard ranked by what each town is built to earn per day.
- **Townsfolk needs** — residents eat grain and bread every tick, and once the town grows they want tools and luxuries too. Every need you supply raises happiness; a town with no food at all starves and slows down. The goods are really consumed, so a big town has to keep producing or buy from other mayors.
- **Radius, not just adjacency** — a park cheers every house within two tiles, industry sours the ones right beside it, and the reach is drawn on the ground while you place.
- **Everything costs goods** — upgrades take coins and resources at every level, so your own chains feed your growth and the market has real demand.
- **Buying is player-only** — the town hall still buys anything at a floor price so you can always cash out, but the only way to buy is another mayor's offer.
- **Roads** — every building's front door (the white arrow while placing) must touch a road, and roads start at the edge of your land. A building cut off from the road stops working and wears a big red **!** until you reconnect or move it. Buildings can be moved for free. Townsfolk stroll the streets.
- **Moods** — happiness is a ladder: Miserable, Uneasy, Content, Happy, Thriving. Each step has visible perks (production, build time, storage), and the happiness bar shows what the town can reach with what it can make right now.
- **Priced by payback** — the first of every building pays for itself in about a week at floor prices; every extra copy costs more, houses most of all. Land goes 50k, 225k, 1M, 4.5M…
- **Tiers take time** — a tier opens once you have housed enough residents and produced enough of the previous tier's goods. Coins buy houses; only tiles and time make goods.
- **Market overview** — the market opens on an overview: a production chart of everything your town makes, and sell 25/50/75/all or "keep X, sell the rest" in one go.
- **Idle timers** — starter buildings still go up in a minute, but every level takes 30% longer than the last, and the higher tiers run for hours: a Bakery is 3h, a Factory 12h, an Emporium a full day. Nothing ever exceeds three days.
- **Goals pay gems** — twenty goals, from your first house to your first Emporium, hand out gems rather than pocket change. Only the last few, worth tens of millions, pay coins.
- **Growth costs goods** — a house is worth two residents a level, and upgrading anything now costs a real slice of what your town makes, not pocket change. Farms and quarries cost wood to put up; houses and lumber camps stay coins-only so a brand-new town can always get started.
